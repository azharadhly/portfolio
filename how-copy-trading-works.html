How cross-broker copy trading works

Copy trading sounds simple: when a strategist trades, followers trade too. The hard parts are deciding whom to trust and placing hundreds of orders at once, inside other people’s broker accounts. FinTruvix builds its whole platform around those two problems.

01One record across brokers

Most copy trading lives inside a single broker, so a strategist’s history disappears when they move. FinTruvix calls itself a cross-broker social trading platform: the record belongs to the trader, not to the broker. That record is the Trader Passport, created with the account, and the site says it can’t be deleted, reset or transferred. It holds:

  • Verified identity. KYC is done once, when the account is created.
  • Roles. Follower, strategist, signalist or programme manager.
  • Score history and conduct record. Every score change and every complaint stays on file.
  • Connected brokers. The trading accounts linked to the passport.

02Connecting a broker

A MetaTrader 5, cTrader or TradeLocker account can be connected in one of two modes. Read-only lets FinTruvix read the trades, which is how a strategist proves a track record. Trade-enabled lets it place copied orders, which is what a follower needs. For MT5, the site says the password is used once to confirm ownership and is never stored.

The money doesn’t move. Capital stays in the follower’s own broker account, and FinTruvix only mirrors trades into it. The one balance it holds is a wallet for top-ups and earnings, and the site says plainly that this wallet is not a bank account.

03One trade, many orders

When a strategist opens a trade, the copy engine has to turn it into a separate order for every follower, each sized for that follower’s account. A managed programme picks one sizing rule:

  • Lot multiplier. 1.00 lot × 0.5 = 0.50 lot.
  • Fixed lot. Always the same size, such as 0.10 lot.
  • Proportional. Scaled to the follower’s allocation, equity or balance. A $2,000 account copying a $20,000 one gets a tenth of the size.

Before anything is sent, risk checks clamp the position size and enforce the allocation cap. A separate monitor pauses copying for anyone who hits a drawdown or daily loss limit. Then every order goes out at the same time.

Speed matters, because a follower whose order lands later gets a worse price. FinTruvix reports a fan-out time of 12 ms at the 99th percentile for 250 followers. That was measured against a mock broker, so real broker execution comes on top, and the design target is 100 ms.

04The Trust Score

Strategies are listed by Trust Score, and the site says nobody can pay to move up. The score has two parts:

  • Performance, P. Profit factor 25%, risk-adjusted return 20%, drawdown management 20%, win rate 15%, reward to risk 10% and longevity 10%. Copier profitability and consistency are declared at 0% until they can be measured.
  • Integrity, I. Built from the conduct record, unresolved complaints and how complete the strategist’s disclosures are.

They combine as T = min(0.6 × P + 0.4 × I, I + 15). The second term is the clever part: no strategist can score more than 15 points above their integrity, however good their returns look.

05How fees work

Followers pay no platform fee on their capital. Strategists set a performance fee, charged on realised profit, and can add a high-water mark so they’re only paid on new highs. No performance fee is charged for a period that ends in a loss. FinTruvix takes its share from the strategist’s earnings.

06AI that can look but not touch

FinTruvix supports the Model Context Protocol, so an AI assistant can read a user’s Trust Score, copy results and the marketplace. The number of tools that can place a trade, change a copy, move money or change a setting is zero. The assistant only sees what the user sees, and only while they allow it.

07Words to know

Trader Passport
A permanent record of one trader: identity, roles, scores and conduct.
Trust Score
A 0 to 100 score that mixes performance with integrity.
Strategist
A trader whose trades others can copy.
Signalist
Publishes trade ideas to a channel that followers can act on.
PAMM
A pooled account where investors hold units in one shared trading account.
High-water mark
A fee rule that only pays on profit above the previous peak.
Drawdown
How far an account falls from its peak before recovering.
Fan-out
Turning one event into many parallel actions, here one trade into many orders.

Based on the public FinTruvix website. Figures are the company’s own; the Trust Score example values are made up.
