How a payment provider works

When a trader taps Deposit, the money passes through four or five companies in a second or two. A payment service provider, or PSP, hides all of them behind one checkout and one API.

01Who’s in the chain

A card payment touches more companies than most people expect.

  • Checkout. The broker’s payment page, often hosted by the PSP so card numbers never touch the broker’s servers.
  • PSP. Collects the payment details and routes them. One integration gives the broker cards, bank transfers and local payment methods.
  • Acquirer. The merchant’s bank. It sends the request into the card network and later pays the money out.
  • Card network. Visa or Mastercard, passing messages between the banks.
  • Issuer. The trader’s bank. It checks the balance and fraud signals, then approves or declines.

Often the issuer first asks for 3-D Secure: a one-time code or a tap in the banking app that proves the card holder is really there.

02Callbacks, not answers

The redirect back to the broker after paying is not proof of payment. The trader can close the tab, and a redirect can be faked. The real answer arrives separately as a webhook: the PSP calls the broker’s server directly with the result, signed so it can’t be forged.

Webhooks are delivered at least once, which sometimes means more than once. A network blip makes the PSP retry, and the same event can arrive twice.

Two habits make this safe:

  • Idempotency. Every event has a unique id. Save it in the same database transaction as the credit, and ignore any id you’ve already seen.
  • Status sync. If a callback never comes, ask the PSP. A scheduled job checks payments stuck in pending and settles them either way.

03Fees, worked out

PSPs usually charge a percentage plus a fixed amount per transaction. For a 100 dollar deposit at 2.5% + $0.30:

Deposit$100.00
Percentage fee, 2.5%$2.50
Fixed fee$0.30
Total fee$2.80
Credited if the client pays$97.20
Credited if the broker pays$100.00

Who pays is a business choice. Either way, calculate in exact decimals and round once, at the end.

04Withdrawals go the other way

A withdrawal, or payout, starts in the back office. Staff or automated rules check it first. Is the account verified? Is the money still needed as margin for open positions? Is it going back to the method it came from? Returning money the way it came in is a standard anti-money laundering rule. Only then does the broker ask the PSP to pay out.

05Reconciliation

Every day the PSP sends a settlement report listing what it actually collected and paid out. Reconciliation matches that report line by line against the broker’s own ledger.

Mismatches are normal. Settlement lags a day or two behind, fees are deducted, refunds and chargebacks arrive later. The goal isn’t zero mismatches; it’s that none goes unnoticed.

06Words to know

PSP
Payment service provider. One API in front of many payment methods and banks.
Acquirer
The merchant’s bank, which processes card payments and settles the money.
Issuer
The bank that issued the card and approves or declines each payment.
3-D Secure
An extra check by the card holder’s bank, such as a code or an app approval.
Webhook
A message the PSP sends to the broker’s server when something happens.
Idempotency key
A unique id that lets the same request be repeated safely.
Settlement
When collected money actually moves to the merchant’s bank account.
Chargeback
The card holder disputes a payment and their bank pulls the money back.
