How a trading platform works

To a trader, a broker is one app. Underneath, it is a handful of separate systems passing messages: a client portal, a back office, a payment provider, the MetaTrader 5 trading server and a liquidity provider. Here is what each one does and how they talk.

01The parts of a broker

Every forex or CFD broker runs some version of the same stack. The names change, the jobs don’t.

  • Client portal. The website or app where people sign up, upload documents, deposit, withdraw and open trading accounts. It is the front door and holds no trading logic itself.
  • Back office (CRM). The broker’s source of truth for people and money: KYC status, wallets, transactions, partners and staff permissions.
  • Payment provider (PSP). Moves real money in and out through cards, bank transfers, e-wallets and crypto, and tells the back office when a payment succeeds. Note 02 covers this.
  • MT5 trade server. Holds trading accounts, balances and open positions, streams prices and executes orders. Traders reach it through the MetaTrader 5 terminal.
  • Liquidity provider. Banks and prime brokers that supply prices and take the other side of the trades a broker passes on.

02How the systems talk

The back office and MT5 don’t share a database. They talk through the MT5 Manager API. When a deposit clears, the back office calls the API to credit the trading account. When a client opens a new account, it creates the account and sets its group and leverage.

Prices travel the other way. Liquidity providers stream quotes through a bridge or gateway into the trade server, which applies the broker’s spread and pushes them to every connected terminal many times a second.

Why two balances?A client usually has a wallet in the back office and a separate balance inside each MT5 account. Moving money between them is an internal transfer, which is how one deposit can fund several trading accounts.

03The life of a trade

  1. Sign up and KYC. Identity documents are checked before any money moves.
  2. Deposit. The PSP charges the card and confirms the payment to the back office.
  3. Fund the trading account. The back office credits the MT5 account through the Manager API.
  4. Open a position. The trader buys 1 lot of EURUSD in the terminal. The trade server checks the margin first.
  5. The market moves. Floating profit and loss update with every price tick.
  6. Close. The realized profit or loss is added to the MT5 balance.
  7. Withdraw. Money goes back to the wallet, then out through the PSP after a review.

04A-book and B-book

When an order reaches the trade server, the broker’s routing rules decide where the risk goes.

  • A-book. The order is passed to a liquidity provider. The broker earns the spread or a commission and carries no market risk.
  • B-book. The broker keeps the order in house and becomes the counterparty. If the client loses, the broker gains, and the other way round.
  • Hybrid. Most brokers mix both, routing by account type, trade size or a client’s track record.

05Where it gets hard

The interesting work happens at the seams: keeping the back office and MT5 in sync when one of them is slow, making sure a payment callback never credits an account twice, and showing each staff member only their own branch’s clients.

Money math matters too. Amounts are stored as whole cents or exact decimals, never as floating point numbers, because 0.1 + 0.2 is not exactly 0.3 to a computer.

06Words to know

Spread
The gap between the buy and sell price. The basic way a broker earns.
Lot
Standard trade size. One lot of EURUSD is 100,000 euros.
Leverage
Borrowed exposure. At 1:100, 1,000 dollars of margin controls a 100,000 dollar position.
Margin
Money set aside to keep a position open.
Manager API
The MT5 interface brokers use to create accounts, move balances and read trades.
Bridge
Software that connects the trade server to liquidity providers.
